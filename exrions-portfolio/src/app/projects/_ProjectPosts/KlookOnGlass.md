Klook On Glass - A glassmorphic redesign of Klook's Mobile App

As part of my module in User Experience Design, we were tasked with redesigning the UI of a mobile app to conform to a UI trend of our choice. I chose to redesign Klook, a travel app, using glassmorphism, the very same style Apple calls Liquid Glass. 
# Introduction
## Brand Choice
We were given several brands/apps to choose from. I chose to cover Klook for my choice of brand. In part, it was due to my love of travelling (specifically to a certain land of the rising sun), but also because its current state was not up to date with Apple's Liquid Glass style, along with many design inconsistencies in the original app. 
## Brand Background & Vision
Klook is an international bookings site for travellers to arrange and purchase tours, attraction tickets, accommodation, transit tickets and more.

The company’s vision is to use sustainable tourism as a force to improve local communities and livelihoods while forging travel experiences with customers.
## Rebrand Strategy
Klook talks a lot about sustainable tourism and supporting locals through tourism. These values are the pillars that my rebrand will be focussed on. 

The platform currently does not display much information regarding the sustainability or environmental impact of their various offered products.

My aim is to then highlight these factors to customers to help guide the app closer to the brand’s vision.

The company is of course, also about tourism as a whole. Which leads to my second point, travel. Klook believes that experiences help to bring the world together, and what says more about travel and experiences than the window?
## Chosen UI Trend
Glassmorphism is a UI Trend that exploded onto the world stage thanks to Apple. Following the release of iOS 26, introducing ‘Liquid Glass’. 

In the modern day, we take all forms of transport to get around, and the thing that separates us from the world as it zips past us is glass.

I chose glassmorphism to further support Klook’s purpose, a travel app. And to bring it into the future to be up to date with the industry leaders of design, Apple.

## Obstacles
One major pattern you might notice with glassmorphic UIs is that they all look a lot better in dark mode. Visibility, contract, all that jazz is much better in general. 

Klook uses a unchanging light-mode UI. Which meant I had to adapt it not only to glassmorphism, but also play around with colour and contrast a lot to get something that looks beautiful yet functional. 

Some of the major settings that had to be dealt with were things like frost, refraction, tinting, and the like. You can see the differences between my tweaks and the default glass settings on Figma. 

The top is the tweaked version, the bottom is the default. Most look fine on an off-white background, but upon testing, they do not hold up to backgrounds with text, images, or any menagerie of colour.
![Glassmorphism Visibility Comparison.](/posts/KlookOnGlass_GlassVisibility.jpg)
Of course, there were many other obstacles, but not one as large as the accessbility of the redesign. 

Small issues such as understanding Figma prototyping functions and their incompatibility with nested components, and the large amount of complex UI elements for the travel app for instance.
# Conclusion
This was a fun project that spanned the entirety of the trimester. Helped me learn a bit of Figma Motion (which had released just a month prior to the final submission), and gave me the oppurtunity to fully utilise components in a larger project. 
# Final Outcome
While the product page is a little buggy due to my hacky workaround of the lack of a scroll-trigger feature, it achieved the effect I wanted it to!
