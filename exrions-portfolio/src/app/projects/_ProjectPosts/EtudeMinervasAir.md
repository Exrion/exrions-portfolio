Etude - Minerva's Air: My third musical composition ever!

Created as part of my final assignment for my Music and Sound Design module at DigiPen, this marked the first long-form music score I have composed that meets my standards, given the circumstances. 
# Introduction
Minerva’s Air is the name I gave the composition I made for the DigiPen animation Etude, directed by Sayah Gold. It features elements of nostalgia, regret, longing and most importantly, rediscovery, resolution and happiness. 
[![Etude Youtube Video](/posts/EtudeMinervasAir_VideoCover.png)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zb117yapXxA)
The animation in question lasts around 3 minutes, with additional time for credits. The composition lasts for around 3 minutes as well. 
# Process
It would be foolish yet true to say that I composed this within a week. Because it really is true, yet untrue. 

Beginning with little music background at the beginning of AY2026 Trimester 3, this module was the one I was most excited about coming into the final trimester of the first year. I was and am ambitious. I look to create things that I am proud of, and things that take my skills and grades to new heights. 

In the beginning, I struggled so much with music composition. Not enough time to learn everything about music theory, but passion drove me still. 
Dissonant and clashing notes were prevalent in my first composition, Fisherman Piping. I learned then, chords, key combinations, scales. 

Then I improved texturing in my second major assignment, to create a seamless BGM for a DigiPen Game. Erudite of Contrarians helped me understand looping music, a little on textures and allowed me to improve further on chords. 

Then came this final assignment. 

Given my early work submissions, I chose to attempt to create a soundtrack, a J-Pop one. It ended in 4 failures, bass lines written but never progressed beyond that, anything I made was unsatisfactory. 

I downscaled then, to orchestral. Still not good enough, clashing brass, low dynamic range, uninteresting use of an orchestral range of instruments. 
So then I began on the composition for Etude, naming it Minerva’s Air. 
# Minerva's Air
The project started rough, too fast I tried to jump into layering instruments. Too often they brough little to nothing to the table. So, I restarted again. 

And with the help of a video by Simon Stevens, where I learned to understand the value and methods involved with using percussion, writing memorable melodies, and using accompaniments. 
[![How to Write Your First Piece of Cinematic Music Youtube Video](/posts/EtudeMinervasAir_VideoCover2.png)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fj3jwdLBD60)
I began writing with two instruments, the grand piano and the violin. Violin playing around C5-C6 and piano around C4-C5. This changed eventually, of course, slightly. 
## Opening
I found myself composing the opening of the animation with the melody first, to find something that was memorable, that carried the emotion of the opening well. 
![Core Melody](/posts/EtudeMinervasAir_ScoreImage.png)
I then wrote a simple piano accompaniment, following my chord progression for the opening (ii7-iii-IV-V7-I7). With the treble clef playing a simple tune and the bass clef playing a 2-minim bass line. Eventually I added a viola playing the main theme of the composition but slowed.

A glockenspiel plays in place of the violin’s melody to represent the innocence and joy of the little girl dancing. 
## Main Theme
This time I wrote things in reverse. I used the piano accompaniment layer to set the tone and mood for the section, something complex emotionally, yet tinged with brightness. I used an arpeggiated pattern for the bass clef, and a simple 4 crochet interval and triad set of notes for each measure.  

I then added the violins to carry the main theme and melody of the piece. Incorporating both melodies to show the change and present and past reminiscing. 

After a fall, the theme reopens with violas and cellos supporting the range of the piece, playing notes from the chord progression (I7-IV7-ii7-V7-vii7). Though this progression isn’t consistent throughout the main theme.

*One thing I would like to note is that none of the chord progressions were chosen prior to composing, the notes came from my throat and mind before being placed. The chords were chosen after I placed them.*
## Climax
![Etude Scary Scene](/posts/EtudeMinervasAir_ScaryScene.png)
As the dancer falls from her past fears haunting her, the music clears out into a drone from the violin. With taikos and bass drums coming in to build towards the climax. A rapidly descending piano scale with dissonant chords to build the atmosphere of fear. 

I then added the violins and piano back, running a build up in intensity and range from the violas and cellos as well. The percussion picked up intensity, and the glockenspiel returns to help finish off the climax. (This sounds oddly inappropriate.)
## Ending
The end is simple. Glockenspiel to represent the little girl again, with the violas coming in to carry the melody of the composition. With the cellos finally providing warmth as the colours of the animation saturate in response to the resolution of the young woman. 
# Conclusion
This journey has made me fall in love with composition and the beauty behind it. I was just an avid audiophile prior to this module, but now I can appreciate better the art behind compositions from my favourite artists. 

I hope to continue composing during my free time. 

I'd like to give my thanks to [Professor Vuk](https://www.linkedin.com/in/vuk-krakovic-33aa227b/) for making time to help me out with composition and sound design despite his busy schedule and my lack of any foundation in music!
